## 1. The 2016–2018 restoration of Y Combinator's Alto (Verdiell / Shirriff / Claunch / Severini et al.)

### Takeaway
Alan Kay gave an **Alto II XM** to Y Combinator after Sam Altman said it would be fun to see one running. From June 2016 a team largely drawn from the CHM IBM 1401 restoration group brought it back: Marc Verdiell (CuriousMarc), Ken Shirriff, Carl Claunch, Luca Severini, Ron Crane and Ed Thelen. It first booted in September 2016. Over 2017–18 it was networked through a new BeagleBone-based 3 Mb/s Ethernet gateway, ran a new Mandelbrot program, and survived repeated Diablo disk-drive crises. The work was documented in a CuriousMarc YouTube series (17+ parts) and a long run of Shirriff blog posts.

### Cited Findings
**Whose Alto and who restored it**
- Sam Altman (YC president) told Alan Kay it would be fun to see an Alto running, and Kay gave him one. The machine was a **Xerox Alto II XM**. The team was Marc Verdiell, Luca Severini, Ron Crane, Carl Claunch and Ed Thelen, plus Ken Shirriff; "special guest" Tim Curley from PARC also helped. — [Shirriff, "Y Combinator's Xerox Alto: restoring the legendary 1970s GUI computer" (June 2016)](http://www.righto.com/2016/06/y-combinators-xerox-alto-restoring.html)
- The team came largely from the IBM 1401 restoration team. — [Shirriff, Day 1 post](http://www.righto.com/2016/06/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day.html); cctalk mailing list announcement "Y Combinator is restoring one of Alan Kay's Xerox Alto machines" (Sept 2016) — [classiccmp.org](https://classiccmp.org/pipermail/cctalk/2016-September/027710.html)
- Hackaday covered the start of the project on June 26, 2016. It noted that working in Silicon Valley meant visits from original Alto and Ethernet people. — [Hackaday, "Restoring The Groundbreaking Xerox Alto"](https://hackaday.com/2016/06/26/restoring-the-groundbreaking-xerox-alto/)
- Ed Thelen kept a separate restoration page, "Restoring a Xerox Alto II Extended". — [ed-thelen.org/RestoreAlto](https://www.ed-thelen.org/RestoreAlto/index.html)

**What broke and how it was fixed (chronological)**
- **Day 1 (June 2016), power supplies and disk interface:** The Alto's power-supply control circuits are built from discrete parts (transistors, op amps, 555 timers), so each supply needs three circuit boards. The YC Alto originally had both a Trident drive and a Diablo drive. When it left service, the Trident drive and the Diablo interface board went elsewhere, leaving a drive that didn't match the interface card. — [Shirriff Day 1](http://www.righto.com/2016/06/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day.html). CuriousMarc's Part 1 video is titled "power supply restoration, disk drive surprise", and one supply was found faulty. — [YouTube, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPyqQXFC2yw)
- **Day 2 (July 2016), display:** The team repaired the CRT monitor. Its circuitry is transistor-based, not IC-based, and it drives the Alto's 606×808 bitmapped display. — [Shirriff Day 2](http://www.righto.com/2016/07/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day.html)
- **Day 3 (July 2016):** The team opened up the Diablo disk drive. — [Shirriff Day 3](https://www.righto.com/2016/07/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day_11.html)
- **Day 4 (July 2016):** A survey of what software was on the system. — [Shirriff Day 4](http://www.righto.com/2016/07/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day_31.html)
- **Day 5 (Sept 2016), smoke:** The team found a broken wire and hooked a logic analyzer up to trace microcode. When they powered on, smoke poured from the backplane: they had mixed up the ground and +5 V pins, and the analyzer pod's ground wire melted. The real reason the machine wouldn't boot turned out to be memory problems. — [Shirriff Day 5](http://www.righto.com/2016/09/xerox-alto-restoration-day-5-smoke-and.html)
- **Day 6 (Sept 2016), one dead gate:** One of the six inverters in a **7414 Schmitt-trigger** chip on the disk interface card had failed, which stopped the disk sector task from running. With no spare 7414 on hand, they used a "dead bug" hack to reroute the signal to an unused inverter. After that, data came off the disk. — [Shirriff Day 6](http://www.righto.com/2016/09/restoring-ycombinators-xerox-alto-day-6.html)
- **The trashed boot disk (Sept 2016):** The boot pack would not boot because someone, decades earlier, had run the Diablo disk exerciser (**DiEx**), which writes random data to the disk and verifies it. Shirriff showed this statistically: byte values on the pack were uniformly distributed, while real Alto sectors are very non-uniform. That was good news, because it meant the hardware wasn't at fault. — [Shirriff, "how our boot disk was trashed with random data"](https://www.righto.com/2016/09/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-how-our-boot.html)
- **Day 7 (Sept 2016):** Experiments with disk and Ethernet emulators. — [Shirriff Day 7](http://www.righto.com/2016/09/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-day-7.html). CuriousMarc Part 7 covered building tools (a BeagleBone Ethernet "dongle", the DiEx disk exerciser) while waiting for a new boot disk. — [CuriousMarc playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL-_93BVApb58I3ZV67LW3S_JEMFnDrQDj)
- **Day 8 (Sept 2016), "it boots!":** After fixing disk-seek problems, the boot screen appeared. Many programs still dropped into the debugger. — [Shirriff Day 8](https://www.righto.com/2016/09/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-day-8-it-boots.html). Gigazine reported the success on Sept 27, 2016. — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20160927-restoring-vintage-xerox-alto/)
- **Day 9 (Oct 2016):** The team traced a crash through software, microcode and hardware. Programs crashed when they tried to execute microcode from the Control RAM, because the microcode-RAM select circuit had been broken by wiring added to the back of the Control board. The CRAM board holds 1024 32-bit microinstructions in 32 memory chips. — [Shirriff Day 9](https://www.righto.com/2016/10/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-day-9-tracing.html)
- **Day 10 (Oct 2016), mouse problems:** The team had no gridded optical mouse pad. Later they found the mouse had the wrong connector: it plugged in but made no electrical contact. — [Shirriff Day 10](http://www.righto.com/2016/10/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-day-10-new.html)
- **Nov 2017, Ethernet board:** The Ethernet board had a chip with a bent pin and another with broken pins. — [Shirriff, "Fixing the Ethernet board from a vintage Xerox Alto"](http://www.righto.com/2017/11/fixing-ethernet-board-from-vintage.html)
- **March 2018, a drive that wouldn't seek:** The Diablo's heads stopped moving. After long study of the head-positioning servo, the fix turned out to be trivial. — [Shirriff, "A 1970s disk drive that wouldn't seek"](https://www.righto.com/2018/03/a-1970s-disk-drive-that-wouldnt-seek.html). Carl Claunch's blog covers the same repair. — [rescue1130.blogspot.com, Mar 2018](http://rescue1130.blogspot.com/2018/03/our-altos-diablo-disk-drive-is-restored.html)
- **May 2018, the "cursed" Diablo:** A Diablo drive sent to CuriousMarc for service by a former Xerox employee destroyed a set of heads and an alignment cartridge. The bent, crashed head dug into the cartridge. New heads crashed so badly that the arms bent. Even once the drive seemed to work, it corrupted every disk put in it. Marc and a partner spent about two months on it, using an FPGA-based tester Carl built after reverse-engineering the disk format, and working out a current adjustment by simulating the circuit in SPICE. — [Hackaday, "Diablo Drive Appears To Be Cursed" (May 10, 2018)](https://hackaday.com/2018/05/10/diablo-drive-appears-to-be-cursed/)

**Getting Ethernet working: the new gateway**
- The Alto's Ethernet runs at 3 Mb/s over coax. The Alto sends TTL signals to an external transceiver and speaks PARC's **PUP** protocols, which predate TCP/IP. None of this is compatible with modern Ethernet. — [shirriff/alto-ethernet-interface README](https://github.com/shirriff/alto-ethernet-interface/blob/master/README.md)
- Shirriff's gateway has three parts. A **BeagleBone Black** "shield" (designed in KiCad) converts signal levels. Code on the BeagleBone's **PRU** real-time microcontrollers speaks the 3 Mb/s Ethernet. PARC's **IFS** (Interim File Server) software, as reimplemented by Living Computers Museum+Labs, runs under Mono on the BeagleBone. With it the Alto can net-boot into the "Net Executive", transfer files by FTP, and load games such as Asteroids. — [alto-ethernet-interface README](https://github.com/shirriff/alto-ethernet-interface/blob/master/README.md); [Shirriff, "Xerox Alto's 3 Mb/s Ethernet: Building a gateway with a BeagleBone" (Jan 2018)](http://www.righto.com/2018/01/xerox-altos-3-mbs-ethernet-building.html)
- **"Breath of Life":** Every second, IFS broadcasts a "breath of life" packet. An Alto net-booting simply listens for it and executes it, so it never has to ask for a boot. This was an original Alto design choice that kept the boot code small. — [Shirriff BeagleBone gateway post](http://www.righto.com/2018/01/xerox-altos-3-mbs-ethernet-building.html); [LCM IFS README (BreathOfLife, EFTP boot, FTP, CopyDisk, gateway, mail; bridging mode lets real Altos talk to emulated ones)](https://github.com/livingcomputermuseum/IFS)
- CuriousMarc **Part 12, "Ethernet pioneers and the first optical mouse"**, features Ethernet inventors and 3Com founders and is dedicated to the memory of Ron Crane. — [YouTube Part 12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XhIohWr10kU); related search summary via [Shirriff Day 10](http://www.righto.com/2016/10/restoring-ycs-xerox-alto-day-10-new.html)

**Demos and highlights**
- **Part 16, "our disk goes down, the Alto connects to Google and draws fractals":** When the Diablo failed, net-booting stood in for the disk. Ken showed network access and a Mandelbrot set. — [HN Theater summary of Part 16](https://yahnd.com/theater/r/youtube/adEr2aRwHnI/)
- **Part 17, "Xerox Alto Demo"** (uploaded Aug 13, 2017): a tour of the Neptune file browser, Bravo, Draw, SIL (circuit editor), and network boot. — [YouTube Part 17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9H79_kKzmFs)

- **Second restoration, 2021 (DigiBarn Alto):** Bruce Damer's DigiBarn Alto II XM (acquired 2003; centerpiece of the Alto's 30th-birthday party in October 2003) was restored in Marc Verdiell's lab. CuriousMarc's series runs "Digibarn Alto Part 1: Another Xerox Alto arrives" through "Part 5: It boots again!". Its README says it is "still under development and is not working yet". — [github.com/shirriff/alto-disk-emulator](https://github.com/shirriff/alto-disk-emulator)

### Inferences
- The restoration arc works well as a scene sequence: power supplies → CRT → smoke → one dead inverter → a disk full of 40-year-old random noise → boot → network via a 2010s single-board computer → a Mandelbrot in an hour. Each step reveals a real piece of Alto architecture: microcode tasks, disk sector task, CRAM, and PUP.
- The Diablo 14-inch pack drive is clearly the machine's weakest part today. That explains why net-booting and disk emulators, including Shirriff's 2025 project, have become central to keeping Altos alive.
- Part 12 is dedicated to Ron Crane, which suggests he died during or soon after the project. I could not confirm his date of death.

## 3. Emulators and running Alto software today (including in a browser)

### Takeaway
**ContrAlto** is the reference Alto emulator. It was written by Josh Dersch at Living Computers: Museum+Labs, released August 2016, and emulates the machine at the microcode level. After LCM closed, development moved to Dersch's **ContrAlto 2.0**, which as of 2026 runs "all known software" on .NET 8. **ContrAltoJS**, a JavaScript port, lets anyone boot Alto disk images in a browser. For Smalltalk, CHM and Dan Ingalls host the **Smalltalk Zoo**, browser-based emulations from Smalltalk-72 through Squeak, including Smalltalk-76.

### Cited Findings
- **ContrAlto launch:** Josh Dersch built it using the CHM Alto archive and Al Kossow's Bitsavers. Paul Allen introduced it on **Aug 2, 2016**, and LCM published the source on GitHub. — [search summary of CHM "A Backup of Historical Proportions" and LCM pages](https://computerhistory.org/blog/a-backup-of-historical-proportions/); [LCM ContrAlto page](https://livingcomputers.org/Computer-Collection/Vintage-Computers/Emulations/ContrAlto.aspx); [LCM Tumblr "The Debut of the ContrAlto"](https://livingcomputers.tumblr.com/post/153206226384/the-debut-of-the-contralto)
- ContrAlto emulates the Alto at the microcode level and also emulates the original Ethernet, so emulated and real Altos can share files, send email and play networked games. — [TechCrunch (Aug 2, 2016)](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/02/living-computer-museum-restores-xerox-alto-and-debuts-new-emulator); [Paul Allen on Medium (Sept 14, 2016)](https://medium.com/vulcan-inc/xerox-alto-is-rebuilt-and-reconnected-by-the-living-computer-museum-e56a7e86be91)
- **ContrAlto 1.x releases** (years not shown on the GitHub page): v1.1 brought performance and bug fixes; v1.2 added macOS/Unix support via Mono + SDL2 and the audio DAC; v1.2.1 added Orbit raster hardware and Dover laser-printer emulation with PDF output; v1.2.2 added the Keyset and Trident disk controller; v1.2.3 added input record and playback scripting. The original repo (AGPL-3.0) is marked "no longer being maintained" and points to `jdersch/Contralto2`. — [github.com/livingcomputermuseum/ContrAlto](https://github.com/livingcomputermuseum/ContrAlto); [releases](https://github.com/livingcomputermuseum/ContrAlto/releases)
- **ContrAlto 2.0** (repo created July 2024 by Josh Dersch). It emulates the Alto I and Alto II XM CPUs; microcode RAM in 1K RAM, 1K RAM/2K ROM or 3K RAM configurations; 256K words of memory; two Diablo 31/44 drives; Ethernet, carried in UDP or raw frames on the host; keyboard, mouse and video; the 5-key **Keyset**; the **audio DAC used by the Smalltalk Music System**; Orbit, Dover ROS and the Dover print engine at **384 dpi** (output to PDF); and the Trident controller with up to eight T-80/T-300 drives. It does not emulate the Diablo printer, serial or IMP interfaces. It needs .NET 8 and runs on Windows, Mac and Unix. It "runs all known software". — [Contralto2 README](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2); [Contralto2 readme.txt](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2/blob/master/Contralto/readme.txt)
- The ContrAlto 2.0 readme describes booting a real Alto: load a 14-inch pack into the Diablo, wait about 20 seconds for it to spin up, then press the **Reset button on the back of the keyboard**. It also says of the 5-key chord keyset, inherited from Engelbart's NLS: "(It never caught on.)" A three-button mouse is "essential" for some software. — [Contralto2 readme.txt](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2/blob/master/Contralto/readme.txt)
- **ContrAlto 2.0 Beta**, "the first Beta release of ContrAlto 2.0", is dated "03 Sep" on GitHub. — [release page](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2/releases/tag/v2.0-Beta)
- **In the browser:** ContrAltoJS is codefrau's JavaScript port of ContrAlto. It is "usable, with some known bugs", beta quality, AGPL-3.0, with a live demo at loomcom.com/contraltojs. — [github.com/codefrau/ContrAltoJS](https://github.com/codefrau/ContrAltoJS). How-To Geek (Oct 12, 2021) walks readers through it: open the site, choose a disk image from the drop-down under the virtual screen, and boot. — [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/752377/the-modern-pc-archetype-use-a-1970s-xerox-alto-in-your-browser/)
- **Other emulators on GitHub:** `lstewart/Alto` (a C emulator, Oct 2014); `alanswx/xerox-dorado`, an emulator for the **Dorado**, the Alto's faster successor (created April 2026). — [GitHub search results](https://github.com/lstewart/Alto); [github.com/alanswx/xerox-dorado](https://github.com/alanswx/xerox-dorado)
- **Networked play with emulators:** *Maze War VR* implements the same PUP protocol as the 1977 PARC Alto Maze War v2.0, so it can play against vintage Alto emulators. — [github.com/marciot/mazewar-vr](https://github.com/marciot/mazewar-vr). A pixel-accurate Alto Maze War recreation also exists. — [github.com/Blueteak/MazeWar](https://github.com/Blueteak/MazeWar)
- **Smalltalk Zoo:** CHM and Dan Ingalls host "the Smalltalk Zoo", marking the 40th anniversary of Smalltalk-80's release. It holds browser-based emulations built in Lively, from Smalltalk-72 to Squeak (begun 1995). It started as background material for Ingalls' HOPL-IV (2020) paper "The Evolution of Smalltalk from Smalltalk-72 through Squeak". — [CHM "Introducing the Smalltalk Zoo"](https://computerhistory.org/blog/introducing-the-smalltalk-zoo-48-years-of-smalltalk-history-at-chm/); [smalltalkzoo.computerhistory.org](https://smalltalkzoo.computerhistory.org/); [Smalltalk-76 page](https://smalltalkzoo.computerhistory.org/welcome.html); [Ingalls paper PDF](https://smalltalkzoo.computerhistory.org/papers/EvolutionOfSmalltalk.pdf)
- Recorded demo: Dan Ingalls demonstrated **Smalltalk-76** live at CHM on Nov 10, 2017. — [YouTube: Smalltalk-76 Demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NqKyHEJe9_w)

### Inferences
- GitHub omits the year for dates in the current year, so "03 Sep" together with a 2026 copyright footer suggests ContrAlto 2.0 Beta came out on **Sept 3, 2026**. Treat this as likely but unconfirmed.
- For viewers who want to "try it": ContrAltoJS (Alto disk images such as Bravo, Draw and games) plus the Smalltalk Zoo (Smalltalk-76) together cover both the Alto OS experience and the Smalltalk GUI experience, with nothing to install.

## 4. Archives: CHM's 2014 Alto release, the 2023 PARC IFS archive, Bitsavers

### Takeaway
On **Oct 21, 2014** CHM, with PARC's permission, released snapshots of Alto source code, executables, documentation and fonts from 1975–1987 at xeroxalto.computerhistory.org, built by Paul McJones's conversion tooling. On **May 10, 2023** it released a much larger PARC IFS archive, almost 150,000 files and about 4 GB covering much of the 1980s, at xeroxparcarchive.computerhistory.org. Al Kossow's media reads and Bitsavers underpin both, and ContrAlto was built from them.

### Cited Findings
- From fall 2013 Paul McJones wrote `restore_alto_files`. It reads archive tape images, unpacks Alto Dump/Load files and disk images, and generates browsable web pages that render Bravo and Press files. The result became **xeroxalto.computerhistory.org**, released in 2014. — [McJones, Dusty Decks "Xerox Alto file server archive" (Oct 24, 2014)](https://mcjones.org/dustydecks/archives/2014/10/24/784/comment-page-1/); [xeroxalto.computerhistory.org](https://xeroxalto.computerhistory.org/)
- On **Oct 21, 2014**, with PARC's permission, CHM made available snapshots of Alto source code, executables, documentation, font files and other files from **1975 to 1987**, as part of its Software Source Code series. — [CHM press release](https://computerhistory.org/press-releases/xerox-alto/); [GlobeNewswire copy](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2014/10/21/675015/10103648/en/Computer-History-Museum-Adds-Historic-Xerox-Alto-Source-Code-to-its-Software-Source-Code-Series.html); [CHM blog "Xerox Alto Source Code"](https://computerhistory.org/blog/xerox-alto-source-code/); coverage in [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2014/10/22/chm_releases_parc_alto_source_code/) and [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1608292/exposed-xerox-alto-and-cp-m-os-source-code-released.html)
- In 2018 PARC and CHM agreed to extend their 2011 agreement to a much larger set of IFS files, covering much of the 1980s. Files are organized by the original PARC server, each shown both as viewable HTML/PDF and as "raw" originals, for non-commercial use. — [xeroxparcarchive.computerhistory.org](https://xeroxparcarchive.computerhistory.org/); [Xerox PARC source code page](https://xeroxparcarchive.computerhistory.org/Xerox_PARC_source_code.html)
- **May 10, 2023 release:** nearly **150,000 unique files, about 4 GB**. Topics include languages, graphics, printing and typography, networking, databases, email, servers, voice, AI, hardware and IC design tools, and additions to the Alto archive. McJones worked under an NDA with PARC from 2018. He read the media Al Kossow had prepared years earlier, updated his Alto conversion software, and cut a list of 300,000 files down to 150,000 for PARC approval. CHM curator **David C. Brock** told the story in "A Backup of Historical Proportions". — [CHM blog](https://computerhistory.org/blog/a-backup-of-historical-proportions/); [McJones, Dusty Decks "Xerox PARC IFS archive" (May 10, 2023)](https://mcjones.org/dustydecks/archives/2023/05/10/1214/); [CHM catalog 300000143](https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/300000143/)
- Al Kossow is a Software Curator at CHM's Shustek Center and the proprietor of **Bitsavers**, a large online repository of historical computer manuals and software. — [CHM interview with Al Kossow](https://computerhistory.org/blog/restoring-yesterdays-computer-system-of-tomorrow-a-conversation-with-curator-alto-restorer-al-kossow/)
- A key Bitsavers document for visuals and architecture is Thacker, McCreight, Lampson, Sproull and Boggs, *Alto: A Personal Computer* (Xerox CSL-79-11). — [bitsavers PDF](http://www.bitsavers.org/pdf/xerox/parc/techReports/CSL-79-11_Alto_A_Personal_Computer.pdf). Bitsavers also has the *Whole ALTO World* newsletters (1977–1980). — [archive.org mirror](https://archive.org/stream/bitsavers_xeroxaltoWtter19771980_26305598/Whole_ALTO_World_Newsletter_1977-1980_djvu.txt)
- DigiBarn keeps its own Alto software pages. — [digibarn.com Alto software](https://www.digibarn.com/collections/software/alto/)

### Inferences
- The preservation chain makes a neat narrative: Kossow reads the old media, McJones converts it into browsable pages, CHM publishes it with PARC's permission, Dersch builds ContrAlto from it, and people run it in the browser via ContrAltoJS. The 2023 archive released three weeks after PARC was handed to SRI (§6).

### Gaps
- The exact 2014 file count wasn't retrieved.
- I didn't confirm whether the Nov 2017 CHM event or the 2023 archive release added new Alto disk images for emulators.

## 5. Museums and surviving Altos: status 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Working or displayed Altos now sit mainly in the US. CHM (Mountain View) displays one in *Revolution* and has restored Altos through its Alto System Project. The Smithsonian's NMAH holds an Alto CPU, monitor, keyboard and games disk. The DigiBarn Alto II XM runs at the System Source Computer Museum in Hunt Valley, Maryland. Seattle's Living Computers: Museum+Labs had restored two Altos in 2016, but closed during COVID and for good in 2024. Its Alto II XM sold at Christie's for $252,000.

### Cited Findings
- **CHM:** The Alto is in the permanent exhibition *Revolution: The First 2000 Years of Computing*. — [CHM Revolution: Xerox Alto](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/input-output/14/347). CHM's software center restored two Altos under its **Alto System Project**, led by Al Kossow. — [CHM Kossow interview](https://computerhistory.org/blog/restoring-yesterdays-computer-system-of-tomorrow-a-conversation-with-curator-alto-restorer-al-kossow/); [HackerNoon/Medium copy](https://medium.com/hackernoon/how-the-center-for-software-history-is-restoring-yesterdays-computer-system-of-tomorrow-a-a7058b1086ca). A restored Alto was used for the live demos on Nov 10, 2017. — [IEEE Spectrum, "The Xerox Alto Struts Its Stuff on Its 40th Birthday"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-xerox-alto-struts-its-stuff-on-its-40th-birthday)
- **CHM entrance:** One IEEE Milestone plaque for the Alto is on the inside face of the front-patio brick wall near CHM's main entrance. — [ETHW Milestone page](https://ethw.org/Milestones:The_Xerox_Alto_Establishes_Personal_Networked_Computing,_1972-1983)
- **Living Computers: Museum+Labs (Seattle):** LCM restored **two** surviving Altos to working order and built a 3 Mb/s bridge to modern PCs. Paul Allen called it the first time Altos had talked to modern PCs, and said the Alto had blown him away when he first saw one about 35 years earlier. — [Paul Allen, Medium (Sept 14, 2016)](https://medium.com/vulcan-inc/xerox-alto-is-rebuilt-and-reconnected-by-the-living-computer-museum-e56a7e86be91); [GeekWire 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/1973-xerox-computer-inspired-paul-allen-others-restored-running-seattle-museum/). Much of the collection belonged to the estate, and some was auctioned by Christie's online from **Aug 23 to Sept 12, 2024** as part of *Gen One: Innovations from the Paul G. Allen Collection*. — [GeekWire, June 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/seattles-living-computers-museum-logs-off-for-good-as-paul-allen-estate-will-auction-vintage-items/); [Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/paul-allens-living-computers-museum-labs-will-close-permanently/); [Wikipedia: Living Computers: Museum + Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Computers:_Museum_+_Labs); [OldVCR blog](http://oldvcr.blogspot.com/2024/06/the-living-computers-museum-finally-isnt.html)
- **The LCM Alto sale:** a **Xerox Alto II XM sold for $252,000** at Christie's. — [GeekWire, auction results](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/auction-of-paul-allen-items-including-vintage-computers-einstein-letter-and-more-brings-in-10m/); [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/paul-allen-computer-collection-10-million-2534740). ## 6. Anniversaries and institutional milestones (2013–2026)

### Takeaway
The Alto has been marked at 40 (2013 and, for Futures Day, 2017) and at 50 (2023). The 2023 anniversary coincided with Xerox handing PARC to SRI International (announced April 24, 2023). CHM held an Alto-at-50 event two days later and released the PARC file archive two weeks after that. IEEE dedicated a triple Milestone (Alto, Ethernet, laser printer) at SRI PARC on May 17, 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Nov 10, 2017, CHM, "Yesterday's Computer of Tomorrow: The Xerox Alto":** held 40 years to the day after Futures Day. Live demos on a restored Alto: Tom Malloy and Charles Simonyi on **Bravo**, Bob Sproull on **Markup** and **Draw**, Doug Brotz on the email client **Laurel**, Dan Ingalls on **Smalltalk-76**, and John Shoch on the Alto's other achievements. — [CHM event page](https://computerhistory.org/events/yesterdays-computer-tomorrow-xerox-alto/); [CHM catalog 102738587](https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102738587); [YouTube full event](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4m_GhapEBLQ); [Bravo demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q_Na1SJXSBg); [Draw & Markup demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GgO7Ww8NXCk); [Shoch presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MP1_BdC4oyM). IEEE Spectrum's takeaway: very little in everyday email, word-processing and graphics software has changed since. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-xerox-alto-struts-its-stuff-on-its-40th-birthday)
- **March 2023:** David C. Brock, "50 Years Later, We're Still Living in the Xerox Alto's World", IEEE Spectrum. — [spectrum.ieee.org/xerox-alto](https://spectrum.ieee.org/xerox-alto); [Techmeme, Mar 2, 2023](https://www.techmeme.com/230302/p3)

- **May 17, 2024:** IEEE dedicated three Milestones at SRI PARC: the Alto ("The Xerox Alto Establishes Personal Networked Computing, 1972–1983"), Ethernet, and the laser printer. — [ETHW Milestone](https://ethw.org/Milestones:The_Xerox_Alto_Establishes_Personal_Networked_Computing,_1972-1983); [IEEE Region 6](https://www.ieeer6.org/2024/04/ieee-triple-milestone-event-50th-anniversary-internet-tcp-ip/); [Forbes (May 25, 2024)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tomcoughlin/2024/05/25/silicon-valley-ieee-milestone-extravaganza/); [ETHW proposal](https://ieeemilestones.ethw.org/Milestone-Proposal:Xerox_Alto)

## 7. Vivid, verifiable anecdotes for scenes (flagged VERIFIED / ATTRIBUTED / DISPUTED / UNVERIFIED)

### Takeaway
The strongest scene material comes from first-person primary sources, especially Alan Kay's HOPL-II history (read in full). It covers Cookie Monster on "Bilbo", Thacker's three-month bet, the "invent it" line said to a Xerox planner, the 12-year-old tool builders, and the Dorado scrolling fix during the Apple visit. Behind these come well-documented events: Futures Day, the Worm, NO MODES and the beanbag room. The famous Jobs "gold mine" line rests on Isaacson's biography, and several popular details (who won the bet, how many machines the Worm crashed, the Futures Day wives quote) vary between sources.

### Cited Findings
**A. Cookie Monster, the first picture (VERIFIED, primary).** Kay: in early April 1973, "just a little over three months from the start", the first Interim Dynabook, called **"Bilbo"**, came up. Within minutes it showed its first bitmap picture: the Muppets' Cookie Monster, which Kay had sketched on the group's painting system. Dan Ingalls then brought Smalltalk across, and for months it was the only software on the machine. — [Kay, *The Early History of Smalltalk*](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). An April 1973 photo shows (left to right) Larry Clark, Chuck Thacker, Alan Kay, Ed McCreight and "Bilbo". — [historyofcg.com Xerox PARC page](https://www.historyofcg.com/pages/xerox-parc/); [Clot Magazine op-ed on Cookie Monster](https://clotmag.com/oped/why-cookie-monster-is-the-mascot-of-big-tech-and-has-been-since-the-1960s-by-lucy-hunter)

**B. Thacker's bet (VERIFIED that the bet happened; outcome DISPUTED).** Kay: Butler Lampson said Thacker "had a bet with Bill Vitic" that he could build "a whole machine in just 3 months". They hoped to "sneak it in" while a hostile Executive "X" was away on a task force. Thacker started on **Nov 22, 1972**. He and two technicians built everything except the disk interface, which Ed McCreight did. Kay's specifications: a 606×808 (about 500,000-pixel) bitmap, about 6 MIPS microcode rate, 128 KB, and 160 MSI chips on two boards. When Executive X came back around Christmas, he tried to kill the project; Lampson wrote a defense of the machine. — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). *Outcome:* Kay implies it ran just over three months later (early April). A secondary source says it took four months and nine days, so Thacker lost. — [Daily Tech News Show (2024)](https://dailytechnewsshow.com/2024/09/05/about-xerox-alto/); see also [IEEE Spectrum "Xerox PARC's Engineers on How They Invented the Future"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/xerox-parc)

**C. "The best way to predict the future is to invent it" (VERIFIED as Kay's own account; wording has precursors).** Kay says he said it, angrily, to **Don Pendery**, Xerox's head "planner", who wanted "trends" and how Xerox could "defend against" the future. The exchange led to the "Pendery Papers for PARC Planning Purposes". — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). Quote Investigator dates it to a 1971 PARC–planner meeting and notes earlier forms: Dennis Gabor, 1963: "futures can be invented". — [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2012/09/27/invent-the-future/)

**D. The name "Alto" (PLAUSIBLE, no primary source found).** Kay calls the machine "Chuck Thacker's Interim Dynabook (later known as the 'ALTO')". — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). Secondary sources say the name comes from Palo Alto / PARC. — [search summary incl. ETHW/CHM](https://ethw.org/Milestones:The_Xerox_Alto_Establishes_Personal_Networked_Computing,_1972-1983). I found no first-hand account of who chose the name or when.

**E. Kids building tools in Smalltalk (VERIFIED, primary).** Kay names **Marian Goldeen** (12), whose painting system was a full tool; **Susan Hamet** (12), with an object-oriented illustration system designed "like the MacDraw to come"; **Bruce Horn** (15), a music-score capture system; and **Steve Putz** (15), a circuit-design system. Kay warns this was "early success syndrome": the children came from Palo Alto schools, which were hardly average. — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). Adele Goldberg took Smalltalk into local schools. — [CHM profile: Adele Goldberg](https://computerhistory.org/profile/adele-goldberg/)

**F. Jobs at PARC, Dec 1979 (primary detail VERIFIED; "gold mine" ATTRIBUTED).**
- Kay's first-hand account: the Smalltalk demo Apple saw ran on a **Dorado**, the Alto's fast "big brother". Its Smalltalk microcode was largely written by **Bruce Horn**, "one of our original 'Smalltalk kids'", still a teenager. Larry Tesler gave the main demo with Dan Ingalls in the "copilot's chair". Jobs disliked the jumpy "blt-style" scrolling and asked for smooth scrolling. "In less than a minute" Ingalls found the methods, changed them, and scrolling was continuous, which shocked the visitors. — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). The same live-edit trick is re-enacted in [Shirriff's Oct 2017 post](https://www.righto.com/2017/10/the-xerox-alto-smalltalk-and-rewriting.html).
- "You're sitting on a gold mine": attributed to Jobs in Walter Isaacson's biography (2011) and widely repeated. — [MakeUseOf summary](https://www.makeuseof.com/xerox-invented-the-future-in-1979-then-handed-it-to-a-24-year-old/); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/silicon-valley-apple-steve-jobs-xerox-437972). Tesler remembered Jobs pacing with a clenched fist under his chin. — [Fortune, raw Tesler footage (2014)](https://fortune.com/2014/08/24/raw-footage-larry-tesler-on-steve-jobs-visit-to-xerox-parc); [Stanford "The Xerox PARC Visit"](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/SUL/sites/mac/parc.html)

**G. Futures Day, Boca Raton, Nov 10, 1977 (VERIFIED; the "wives" detail is widely reported and its quote attribution UNCERTAIN).** More than 300 Xerox executives and their wives attended a sales conference. PARC used Hollywood set builders to make **four mock cubicles**. It flew **20 Altos and 6 laser printers** to Florida in space leased on **two DC-10s**. More than 100 staff worked on the show and about 50 went. The executives' reaction was lukewarm. — [IEEE Spectrum, "Behind the scenes at Xerox PARC's Futures Day"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/behind-the-scenes-at-xerox-parcs-futures-day40-years-ago). The male executives reportedly ignored the machines while their wives tried the mouse and typed, because typing was seen as secretaries' work. The exact wording of the quote attributed to Taylor is uncertain. — [Wikipedia "Office of the future"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_future); [Samira Ahmed blog](https://samiraahmed.blog/the-curse-of-the-typing-pool/)

**H. The Worm that ate the network overnight (VERIFIED in essence; scale DISPUTED).** Shoch and Hupp's worms were multi-segment programs that searched out idle Altos, copied themselves in, and repaired themselves when a segment's host was rebooted. — [Shoch & Hupp, CACM 25(3):172–180, March 1982](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/358453.358455). In one early test a worm was left running overnight on a few machines. By morning dozens of machines had crashed, some in locked offices, and the worms were stopped by injecting a special packet telling them to stop. — [gunkies.org "Worm"](https://gunkies.org/wiki/Worm); [John Menick, "The Conquering Worm"](https://www.johnmenick.com/writing/the-conquering-worm.html). Another retelling says "more than a hundred machines". — [FKA Witte de With essay](https://www.fkawdw.nl/en/review/sediments/the_conquering_worm_part_1). Use "dozens"; that wording follows the original paper's account as summarized.

**I. Mazewar and Alto Trek (VERIFIED that the games existed; network-shootout specifics UNVERIFIED).** Maze War began at NASA Ames in 1973 (Steve Colley, Greg Thompson, Howard Palmer). PARC's Alto remake, **version 2.0 (1977)**, used PUP over Ethernet. — [mazewar-vr README](https://github.com/marciot/mazewar-vr). The Alto Mazewar is credited to Jim Guyton, Mike Wahrman and colleagues. — [Wikipedia, Maze (1973 video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maze_(1973_video_game)). **Alto Trek**, by Gene Ball and Rick Rashid as grad students at the University of Rochester in the late 1970s, was one of the first networked multiplayer games: each player flies a ship from their own Alto as a Klingon, Romulan or Terran. — [Wikipedia, Alto Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_Trek). The restored DigiBarn Alto ran it in 2021. — [Shirriff on X](https://x.com/kenshirriff/status/1398346757424095234)

**J. Larry Tesler's NO MODES plate (VERIFIED).** Tesler put a "NO MODES" vanity plate on his cars from the early 1980s until his death in 2020, one of them a Dodge Valiant. He wore a "Don't Mode Me In" T-shirt, and his Twitter handle was @nomodes. — [Wikipedia, Mode (user interface)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_(user_interface)); [Fast Company tribute](https://www.fastcompany.com/90466328/a-tribute-to-larry-tesler-the-father-of-user-friendly-design); [photo post, Buzz Andersen](https://buzz.tumblr.com/post/76954831925/apple-vp-larry-teslers-no-modes-license-plate)

**K. Taylor's beanbag room and "Dealer" (VERIFIED).** Lab director Bob Taylor held regular informal meetings in CSL's "beanbag" conference room, where staff presented ideas and got frank, sometimes brutal, feedback. "Dealer" was Taylor's way of running the lab without a formal management structure. — [CHM Revolution: The PARC Computer Science Laboratory](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/input-output/14/348/1868); [Wikipedia, Robert Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Taylor_(computer_scientist))

**L. The Alto could sing (VERIFIED, primary).** Kay's sampling-synthesis music produced 3 real-time voices on a Nova. Bob Shur and Chuck Thacker moved it to the Alto and got **12 real-time voices**. — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/). ContrAlto 2.0 emulates the audio DAC used by the "Smalltalk Music System". — [Contralto2 readme](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2/blob/master/Contralto/readme.txt)

**M. The boot ritual (VERIFIED).** Slide a 14-inch pack into the Diablo, wait about 20 seconds for spin-up, then press **Reset on the back of the keyboard**. The screen goes white, and within 5–10 seconds a cursor appears, followed by the Executive banner. — [Contralto2 readme](https://github.com/jdersch/Contralto2/blob/master/Contralto/readme.txt)

**N. "Let's Burn Our Disk Packs" (VERIFIED, primary).** A three-day LRG offsite with that name. Kay argued that "no biological organism can live in its own waste products" and pushed for a fresh start beyond the Alto and Smalltalk. — [Kay, HOPL-II](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/)

**O. Restoration-era moments (all VERIFIED; see §1–2).** Smoke from a mis-wired logic analyzer. A "dead bug" repair of a single dead inverter. A boot disk found full of random bytes from a decades-old DiEx test. The "breath of life" packet arriving once a second. A 45-year-old password hash inverted, with its original owner turning up in the comments. A Mandelbrot taking an hour, then 9 minutes. A "cursed" Diablo destroying heads and an alignment pack. An Alto on the modern internet talking to Google.

**P. Scale and reach (REPORTED by a secondary source).** More than 2,000 Altos were built across Alto I and II, and none were sold commercially. Xerox donated 50 to US universities in 1979, and several were used in the Carter White House. — [How-To Geek (2021)](https://www.howtogeek.com/752377/the-modern-pc-archetype-use-a-1970s-xerox-alto-in-your-browser/). Also: by 1981 Xerox had installed 75 Ethernets at 40 sites serving over 1,350 Altos. — [search summary incl. ### Inferences
- **Suggested opening:** a white portrait screen goes on in April 1973 and Cookie Monster appears on "Bilbo". It is primary-sourced, visual, and immediately connects to the Alto's child-centered, Dynabook origins.
- **Suggested closing options:** (1) a 2016 restorer presses Reset on the back of the keyboard and the Executive banner appears 43 years on; (2) the April 2023 week when Xerox gave PARC away and the Alto's designers met at CHM for its 50th; (3) anyone in 2026 booting Bravo in a browser tab via ContrAltoJS.
- **The Apple-visit scene is stronger, and more accurate, told through Kay's account:** Ingalls rewriting scrolling in under a minute, on a Dorado. That avoids overclaiming the "gold mine" line, and the Dorado detail corrects a common "Jobs saw an Alto" simplification. Apple engineers are also reported to have seen Altos on the visits, but my sources didn't settle exactly which machine was used for which demo.

### Gaps
- Specific in-period Mazewar anecdotes (e.g., PARC restricting play because of network load, the eyeball avatars) were not verified.
- I found no first-hand source for the Alto's "sound on the demo floor", meaning the fans and Diablo drive noise. There is anecdotal knowledge that the Diablo is loud, but no citation.
- I didn't verify whether Rick Rashid later led Microsoft Research; it is widely known but was not checked here.
- Kay's "Kay's own kids" (as opposed to the school children) using Smalltalk: no source found. The verified material concerns children from Palo Alto schools.

## 8. Visual references for original JS-drawn visuals (described, not copied)

### Takeaway
The Alto's look is easy to rebuild in code: a portrait, paper-white 606×808 bitmap; a three-button mouse; a 5-key chord keyset; a floor cabinet with a 14-inch Diablo pack loading from the front; and interfaces like Bravo, Draw and Smalltalk-76's overlapping windows. Good reference material includes CHM's photos, the April 1973 "Bilbo" photo, CSL-79-11 diagrams, the CuriousMarc and CHM demo videos, and the Smalltalk Zoo running live.

- **Display:** 606×808 portrait bitmap (about 500k pixels) with proportional fonts and graphics; black on white. — [Shirriff Day 2](http://www.righto.com/2016/07/restoring-y-combinators-xerox-alto-day.html); [Kay](https://worrydream.com/EarlyHistoryOfSmalltalk/).
